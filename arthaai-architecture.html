The three laws that make it "ArthaAI"

Break any of these and it becomes a different, worse system. They're the reason the architecture is auditable rather than a black box.

LAW 1
Numbers are deterministic; the LLM only explains.
Every figure that sizes a trade is computed in code and backtestable. The model interprets and narrates — it never forecasts a price or invents a probability. This is also why fine-tuning on price data is the wrong tool.
LAW 2
A reproducible graph, not an agent free-loop.
The same nodes fire in the same order every run — predictable cost, auditable path. It's the deliberate opposite of an open-ended agent that decides its own steps, and it's the defensible ground versus research playgrounds.
LAW 3
Nothing is trusted until it beats buy-and-hold.
The backtest gate governs everything downstream. A signal — or a new factor, or a new data source — earns its place only by beating a passive benchmark out of sample. No exceptions, no real money before it.

The one thing to build next

Everything above runs. But the system has one missing piece, and it's the only one that matters right now.

★ Priority — the Quant Agent's factor engine

Turn ~3 indicators into a real signal.

The plumbing is done end to end; what it lacks is edge — today's naive signal underperforms simply buying and holding. The fix isn't a better prompt or a bigger model; it's a stronger deterministic signal. Bring in the published alpha-factor families (Alpha101, GTJA191, Qlib158), combine them with a tabular model, and let the backtest gate judge it. It slots into the deterministic layer with zero architectural change — and it's the difference between "impressive demo" and "actually useful."

Alpha factors → tabular model (XGBoost) → signal → backtest gate → (only if it wins) → live use

Why this is the right architecture for ArthaAI

Competitors are research playgrounds — flexible agent loops that can do anything, explain nothing the same way twice, and cost differently each run. ArthaAI's edge is the opposite: a reproducible, auditable decision engine where the maths is separable from the model, the sizing is capped by hard policy, and every claim is backtest-gated. That's not the widest product — it's the one a risk committee can sign off on. Keep it narrow, prove the edge, and that becomes a genuine moat.
