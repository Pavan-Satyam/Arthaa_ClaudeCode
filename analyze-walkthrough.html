The journey, step by step

Each card says what happens in plain language. The grey strip underneath shows which part of the code runs and where the data comes from.

1

Your click leaves the browser

The web page sends a request to our own server saying "analyse GLD". You don't enter any password — the page was already handed a secure key when it loaded, so it just works.

Under the hood runs dashboard.html → analyze() sends POST /analyze/GLD
2

Security check at the door

The server checks the key is valid and that you're not hammering it with requests. Bad key, no entry. This is the same gate an outside app would have to pass.

Under the hood runs gateway/auth.py, gateway/app.py key from HashiCorp Vault
3

Fetch the price history — this is where data enters

If we've never seen GLD before, the system registers it and downloads roughly two years of daily prices from the market data source, then stores them in our own database so future runs are instant. This is the slow part on a brand-new ticker (a few seconds).

Under the hood runs data/ingest.py → ingest() fetches from Yahoo Finance (free) stores in TimescaleDB
4

Four specialists work at the same time

Rather than queuing, all four run in parallel — which is why the whole thing takes seconds, not a minute. Before any of them can touch data, a policy engine checks that that specific specialist is allowed to use that specific tool. The news reader, for example, has no permission to place trades.

Running simultaneously

Chart Reader
Trend, moving averages, momentum — from our price database.
Statistician
Return, volatility, win rate — the maths the risk manager needs.
News Reader
Headlines filtered to GLD only, then scored for mood.
Satellite Watcher
Physical signals. Placeholder today; skips equities entirely.
Under the hood runs agents/db_agent, quant_agent, news_agent, alt_agent reads TimescaleDB + Qdrant permissions by Open Policy Agent
5

Everything lands on one shared desk

All four findings are collected into a single shared record. Nothing is lost or summarised away — the strategist sees the complete picture, including where the specialists disagree.

Under the hood runs orchestration/graph.py (LangGraph) shared state: GraphState
6

The strategist makes the call — and explains it

The AI model reads the full picture and writes a verdict: bullish, bearish, or neutral, a confidence level, and a written rationale that must end with "I choose this because…". It's required to flag conflicting evidence rather than paper over it.

If the AI is unavailable, the system automatically falls back to the next option — a local model, or a built-in rule-based reasoner. It never returns nothing.

Under the hood runs agents/master_llm.py → reason() calls Google Gemini 2.5 Flash fallback: local model → offline reasoner
7

The risk manager sizes the position

A century-old formula (the Kelly Criterion) converts the odds into "what fraction of the portfolio is this worth?" — then deliberately takes only a quarter of that to smooth out the bumps, and finally caps it at 5% of the portfolio. That cap is absolute: the AI cannot argue its way past it.

Under the hood runs agents/asset_manager.py → size() quarter-Kelly + hard 5% policy cap
8

The answer comes back and the screen fills in

The verdict, the reasoning, the numbers behind it, and the suggested position size all travel back to your browser, and the price chart redraws. If the ticker doesn't exist, you get a clear "no market data" message instead of a blank screen.

Under the hood returns {verdict, allocation, evidence} rendered by dashboard.html

The safety rails

Designed on the assumption that an AI can be wrong or manipulated. Every limit below is enforced by code, not by asking the model nicely.

Hard position cap
No single asset can exceed 5% of the portfolio, whatever the AI concludes.
Paper trading only
Nothing reaches a broker. Orders are simulated; no real money can move.
Automatic stop-loss
Every simulated position carries a pre-set exit level.
Daily loss circuit-breaker
A 10% daily drawdown halts all trading outright.
Least-privilege agents
Each specialist can only touch its own tools — checked on every single call.
Never fails silently
If a data source or the AI goes down, the system degrades to a backup and says so.

The one thing that matters most

The plumbing works end to end — but the underlying trading signal has not yet been shown to beat simply buying and holding. Our own backtest says it currently underperforms. That's the honest headline: this is a working, well-built platform waiting for a proven edge, not a money-making system. Improving that signal is the next real milestone — and nothing should touch real money before it.

Why this matters, and where it could go

The differentiator isn't the prediction — plenty of tools guess a direction. It's that every call arrives with its reasoning, its sources, and its position size attached, and that the maths is separated from the AI so it can be independently checked. A black-box signal can't be audited; this can.

Explains itself
Every verdict states the evidence it used and where it conflicts — reviewable by a human before anything happens.
Numbers ≠ AI
All maths is deterministic and testable. The model interprets; it never invents a figure that sizes a trade.
Swappable brains
Gemini today, a local private model tomorrow — one config line. No vendor lock-in, and data can stay in-house.

The investment ladder

Each rung buys a specific capability. Nothing needs to be bought until the rung below has paid off. Figures are rough planning estimates, not quotes.

StageRough monthly costWhat it unlocks
Today ~$0 Everything you just saw — any US ticker, on demand, on a laptop. Free data, free AI tier, self-hosted databases.
Hosted ~$40–100 Runs in the cloud instead of a laptop, so it's always on and shareable with a small team.
Live news ~$50–200 A real news wire and proper sentiment scoring, replacing today's sample data. Sentiment becomes genuinely tradeable input.
Whole-market scan ~$1k–5k Bulk price data for all ~12,500 listed names — shifts the product from "analyse one ticker" to "rank the entire market every morning". This is the biggest product leap.
Physical intelligence $5k–40k+ Satellite and cargo feeds (oil in storage, ships at sea). The genuine information-asymmetry edge in commodities — and the reason the Satellite Watcher exists as a placeholder today.
The gate before any spending

Every rung above is wasted money until one thing is true: the signal must beat buying and holding, out of sample. It doesn't yet. The right sequence is to improve the signal on free data — where iteration costs nothing — and only then buy the data that amplifies a proven edge. Spending first would mean paying to scale something that doesn't work.
